    RD-03What you're looking at

    The camera that couldn't see.

    Writer and photographer · Substack · 2026
    1. On Apollo 11, one camera went outside onto the Moon. It rode on a bracket on Neil Armstrong's chest.
    2. It flew without a viewfinder. Crews trained on Earth to aim by feel, pointing their whole body at the subject. That's why the finder hood lifts away as the view pulls back.
    3. A glass réseau plate near the film pressed a grid of small crosses onto every frame, so scientists could measure what the pictures showed. The crosses behind the drawing come from that grid.
    Sources: NASA Apollo Lunar Surface Journal; Hasselblad; NPR, July 2019. The figure and camera are generic drawings, not a portrait or a product replica.
